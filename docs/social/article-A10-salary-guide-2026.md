# The UK Private Healthcare Salary Guide 2026

*By discipline. The page candidates bookmark and practices benchmark against. Big SEO play — update annually. ~1,300 words.*

> **A note on the numbers:** these are indicative 2026 ranges for **private-practice** roles in the UK, not the NHS. Real pay varies with location (London and the South East run higher), experience, patient volume, and whether commission or profit-share is on top. Use them as a benchmark, not a rulebook. Where a role commonly carries commission, we say so — because for several of these, the base is only half the story.

---

## Why this guide exists

Two of the most common questions in private healthcare hiring are the same question from opposite sides of the table. Practices ask: *"What do I have to pay to actually fill this?"* Clinicians ask: *"Am I being underpaid?"*

Both deserve a straight answer instead of "competitive." So here it is, discipline by discipline.

## Aesthetics & MedSpa

The fastest-moving pay in private healthcare — and the one where base salary tells you least, because commission and a personal client following can double a package.

| Role | Indicative base (2026) | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Aesthetic nurse (non-prescriber) | £30,000–42,000 | Often + commission |
| Nurse prescriber / injector | **£38,000–58,000** | + commission; the in-demand role |
| Aesthetic doctor | £60,000–110,000+ | Sessional/day rates common |
| Advanced aesthetic practitioner | £32,000–48,000 | Rising as regulation tightens |
| Clinic manager | £30,000–45,000 | + performance bonus |
| Patient coordinator | £24,000–32,000 | + commission on conversions |

**The honest read:** if you're an aesthetic nurse prescriber sitting at the bottom of the band with no commission structure, you're subsidising the clinic. Regulation tightening through 2026 is pushing demand for *registered* practitioners up — which means leverage is moving toward the clinician.

## Dentistry (for reference — inbound only)

Included because candidates search it. Associate dentists typically work on a UDA or percentage basis rather than salary, with earnings ranging widely (commonly £60,000–130,000+ depending on list and private mix). Dental nurses: £24,000–32,000. Hygienists/therapists: £30,000–45,000 or sessional.

## Five things every salary conversation should cover

A number on its own is half a deal. Before you accept — or before you make — an offer, pin down:

1. **Is there commission or profit-share?** For aesthetics, optometry and some physio roles, it's most of the package.
2. **Who covers indemnity?** A £2–4k difference that quietly moves the real value of the offer.
3. **What's the CPD budget?** £500–2,000/year is healthy. Below £500 is a red flag about how they value you.
4. **What are the appointment lengths / list sizes?** Pay per hour worked matters more than the headline.
5. **Is the band real or aspirational?** "Up to £X" usually means the X is for someone who doesn't exist yet.

## For practices: what underpaying actually costs

It's tempting to anchor low. But in a shortage market the maths is unforgiving: a band that's £3–5k under market doesn't save you money — it filters out the confident candidates, attracts the ones who can't get hired elsewhere, and lands you back in a re-advertise cycle that costs far more than the gap you tried to save. Pay the market, name the number, win the hire once.
